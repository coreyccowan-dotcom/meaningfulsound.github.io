PedalEditor

Reference

PedalEditor is a wireless editor for MIDI guitar pedals. From an iPhone, iPad or Mac it recalls presets and edits parameters over Bluetooth LE MIDI or Wi-Fi, using Program Change and Control Change — nothing plugged into the board, nothing to crouch over. USB is there if you would rather be wired. It works with pedals from any brand, including the ones the manufacturer never made an editor for.

What it does

  • Recalls a preset on a pedal with a Program Change, plus Bank Select on CC 0 and CC 32 where the pedal banks its slots.
  • Sets any documented parameter with a Control Change message, with the pedal's real range and value curve.
  • Sends MIDI Clock or a tap-tempo CC to pedals that follow tempo.
  • Keeps presets for every brand in one library on the phone, not in numbered slots scattered across pedals.
  • Recalls a whole board at once, by song and song section, and runs a setlist in order.
  • Reads the current preset back off the hardware where the manufacturer publishes a SysEx dump — Strymon BigSky, TimeLine and Mobius.
  • Syncs across your own devices through your personal iCloud account, and exports everything as files.

How it connects

  • Bluetooth LE MIDI — paired inside the app on iPhone and iPad, in Audio MIDI Setup on Mac. The link is owned by the operating system, so it reconnects on its own.
  • Wi-Fi — RTP-MIDI network sessions, which appear as ordinary MIDI destinations.
  • USB — any class-compliant Core MIDI interface, on iPad or Mac.

An iPhone can also drive a Mac running PedalEditor over the local network, leaving the Mac connected to the rig.

The four tabs

TabWhat it is
EditorOne pedal's full control surface. Every parameter its manual documents, drawn as the control it actually is — knob, fader, toggle, selector, stepper or list — not a wall of generic sliders. Turn a control and the CC goes out immediately. Save the result to a preset.
LibraryEvery preset you have made, across every brand, in one place. Presets group into collections and banks and are scoped by project, so a session rig and a band rig do not sit in the same list. Preset names live in the app, so they are not limited to the pedal's own display.
SongsA song holds the state of every pedal on your board at once. Song sections — verse, chorus, solo — each carry their own preset per pedal. Setlists are ordered runs of songs and are built here.
LiveThe only place anything is deployed to hardware. Walk a setlist section by section; each advance sends the right preset to every pedal on the board. One action changes the whole rig.

Questions

Can I control my Strymon BigSky from my iPhone?

Yes, wirelessly. Pair the pedal's MIDI interface over Bluetooth LE inside the app, then edit parameters and recall presets from the phone. The BigSky, TimeLine and Mobius can also send their current preset back to the app over SysEx, so you can capture what is already on the pedal.

Is there an app for Walrus Audio, Meris or UAFX pedals?

Not from those manufacturers for most of their boxes. PedalEditor provides one, built from each pedal's published MIDI implementation.

Do I need a MIDI controller pedal on the floor?

No. PedalEditor is the controller. You need a way for MIDI to reach the pedal — a Bluetooth LE MIDI adapter, a network MIDI session, or a USB interface — but no dedicated footswitch controller.

How does it connect?

Bluetooth LE MIDI, Wi-Fi using RTP-MIDI network sessions, or USB through any class-compliant Core MIDI interface. On iPhone and iPad, Bluetooth pairing happens inside the app. On Mac it is done in Audio MIDI Setup.

What does it actually send?

Program Change to recall a preset slot, Bank Select on CC 0 and CC 32 where the pedal banks its slots, and Control Change messages for parameters. MIDI Clock and tap-tempo CCs where the pedal supports tempo.

Do I have to build a profile for my pedal?

No. Profiles are built from the manufacturer's manual and audited before release. For a MIDI device with no dedicated profile, the included 128 Knobs profile gives you general-purpose CC control.

Does it need an internet connection?

No. Sync between your own devices uses your personal iCloud account when it is available. There is no account to create, no analytics, and no server belonging to us in the path.

Facts

  • Platforms: iPhone and iPad on iOS/iPadOS 17, Mac on macOS 14 Sonoma. All native; no web wrapper.
